Expanding-window rolling origin: 24 origins × 12 horizons, every method scored on identical origins. MASE is scaled by the in-sample seasonal-naive error, so it is comparable across series and the relative ordering is what carries meaning. Gradient boosting is 17% worse than the naive baseline on 204 monthly observations — more model capacity than the data can identify. SARIMA and Holt-Winters both earn their complexity; the modern method does not.

400 real outlets, clustered on rates rather than totals (sales per m², margin, hours per m², investment per m²), which makes the features size-neutral by construction. The productive segment is 2.3× ahead on sales per m² — and is the smaller-footprint group. Margins are within a point of each other across segments, so this is an operations and staffing story, not a pricing one.

The ask is usually micro-segments; the evidence here supports two. Silhouette drops sharply from k=2 to k=3 and never recovers, so cutting finer is a presentation choice rather than a structural one. At k=5 the smallest segment holds 1.8% of the estate — seven stores, too few to run a programme against — and stability falls from 0.79 to 0.65 adjusted Rand across 40 bootstrap refits.

Is it just size in a costume? Store size explains 14% of the variance in segment membership. Under the 50% bar, so the segmentation carries information a size ranking does not. This is the check that most segmentation decks skip.

Scope, stated rather than buried

Two real public datasets, each matched to one question: 204 months of monthly demand (1991–2008) for the forecasting, and 400 Dutch menswear outlets (1990, Ecdat::Clothing) for the segmentation. They are stand-ins for a retail planning problem, not evidence about any particular retailer. The forecast comparison is one series, so it shows that capacity must match data, not that boosting always loses. Two of the 48 model fits raised convergence warnings and were kept rather than dropped, since silently discarding the hard origins is how a backtest flatters itself.
